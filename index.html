Welcome to my personal homepage

I am an M.Sc. student in Computer Science and Engineering at Daffodil International University, Dhaka, Bangladesh, where I also completed my B.Sc. in Computer Science and Engineering. My M.Sc. thesis, NEMA-Q, develops a hyperbolic quantum-classical graph neural network with observable-level explainability; the accompanying paper has been accepted at ICEQT'26 for publication in Springer Nature proceedings.

My research sits at the intersection of quantum information science and machine learning. I build simulation-first, reproducible pipelines, and I care about results that survive scrutiny — which means reporting negative results, seed variance, and honest baselines alongside the headline numbers.

My current work spans several threads:

  • Quantum machine learning and explainability — hybrid quantum-classical graph networks, quantum observable attribution, barren-plateau-free architectures, and quantum reservoir computing audited against matched classical surrogates.
  • QKD security — showing that QBER monitoring is structurally blind to photon-number-splitting and detector-blinding attacks, and that learned detectors over observables a decoy-state receiver already computes close that gap with no added hardware.
  • Quantum error correction and networking — logical teleportation between heterogeneous QEC codes, seam-aware decoding across QPU boundaries, and buffer-management policies for quantum-repeater entanglement memories.
  • Quantum metrology and certification — adaptive entanglement allocation for distributed sensing under drifting noise, and SDP-based genuine multipartite entanglement witnesses.

I work independently, using open tooling and free compute. If you share an interest in any of the above, please reach out — I am always open to collaboration.
